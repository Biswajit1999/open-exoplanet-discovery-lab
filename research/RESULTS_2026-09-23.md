# Validated public-data results — 23 September 2026

## Scope

This report records the completed public-data analyses in release 0.3.0. It
separates archive measurements, fitted diagnostics and synthetic validation.
It contains no new-planet claim.

## Public archive snapshot

The frozen snapshot contains 6,366 confirmed-planet rows, 8,148 TOI rows and
1,826 NASA Exoplanet Archive atmospheric-spectrum records for 289 planets. The
atmosphere count is a product count, not a count of independent observations.

VizieR catalogue `J/AJ/170/264` supplied 5,920 NETS III RV rows and 5,920
activity rows for 41 survey stars. Catalogue `J/AJ/171/286` supplied 93 public
NEID RVs for the HD 190360 control.

## NETS III descriptive diagnostics

Across all 41 NETS III targets, the median target has 92 RV epochs, a
1,001.95 d baseline, 0.34 m s⁻¹ quoted internal uncertainty, 2.062 m s⁻¹ raw
weighted RMS and 1.794 m s⁻¹ run-demeaned weighted RMS. The median WRMS change
is 13.05%. Thirty-five targets have a between-run weighted-mean span above
1 m s⁻¹ and 13 have a WRMS change above 20%.

These are sensitivity diagnostics. Run means may contain instrumental offsets,
activity, long-period companions and sampling effects; run de-meaning can also
absorb real signals.

## NETS III injection/recovery

Forty stars have at least 20 complete RV, uncertainty, run and S-index rows,
leaving 5,782 measurements in the completeness experiment. Three declared
models were compared:

1. one global offset;
2. run offsets plus per-run white jitter;
3. run offsets, per-run jitter and a linear S-index term.

The grid used periods of 5, 10, 30, 100, 300 and 500 d; circular
semi-amplitudes of 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3 and 5 m s⁻¹; six phases per cell; and 100
deterministic residual permutations per target and model to set the recovery
threshold.

The population mean of `C(era+activity) - C(baseline)` is +0.0205, but that
single number hides the main result. The activity-aware model improves recovery
at short periods and loses sensitivity at 500 d. The largest absolute grid-cell
difference is 0.4542, at 500 d and 5 m s⁻¹, where the era/activity projection
removes long-timescale signal power.

Population K50 values (m s⁻¹) are:

| Period | Baseline | Run offsets | Run + activity |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 d | 2.00 | 1.57 | 1.43 |
| 10 d | 1.98 | 1.58 | 1.36 |
| 30 d | 1.93 | 1.56 | 1.43 |
| 100 d | 2.15 | 1.77 | 1.52 |
| 300 d | 2.38 | 4.04 | 3.80 |
| 500 d | 2.58 | not reached | not reached |

K90 is not reported as a number because the tested 0.5–5 m s⁻¹ amplitude grid
does not bracket 90% population recovery. This is an explicit bound, not a
missing result.

Leave-one-run-out nuisance projection retains a median 98.6% of a 5 d signal,
95.3% at 100 d, 88.4% at 300 d and 89.2% at 500 d. The worst-phase median falls
to 69.6% at 500 d, with individual held-out combinations lower still. The
result supports model-specific completeness rather than applying activity/run
corrections as a generic cleaning step.

## ESO NIRPS × HARPS census

A complete live ESO TAP query returned 34,751 public NIRPS products; the
independent count query returned the same number. The products group into 852
archive target identities. Coordinate queries found public HARPS products for
696 identities, 662 with at least one NIRPS/HARPS epoch pair within one hour and
664 within one day. All 852 coordinate queries completed with zero errors.

These are archive target labels, not a deduplicated stellar catalogue, and the
counts describe products/epochs rather than extracted homogeneous RVs. A
precision optical/NIR amplitude comparison still requires product-level FITS
inspection, duplicate handling and NIRPS DRS/PROCSOFT verification. Products
from the documented DRS 3.2.6 affected interval must not be used unless their
corrected reprocessing is verified.

## TESS temporal activity context

Public SPOC 120 s light curves for HD 10780 were divided into control sectors
24–25 and later test sectors 85–86. The explicit quality rule is `QUALITY == 0`,
finite positive uncertainties, a 7-MAD flux clip and 30-minute weighted bins.

The strongest generalized Lomb–Scargle periods are 5.762 d in the control and
6.094 d in the test. Their analytic false-alarm probabilities are extremely
small, but within-sector circular-shift calibration gives p = 0.950 for the
control peak and p = 0.0149 for the test peak. The fixed-control-period phase
difference is -1.716 rad and the test/control fitted amplitude ratio is 1.113.

This mismatch between analytic and time-correlated nulls is the result: the
5.8–6.1 d structure is useful activity context, but is not labelled a planet or
a secure stellar-rotation period.

## Atmospheric reproducibility

The metadata census contains 245 planets with multiple spectrum records, 149
with multiple publications and 172 with multiple instruments. Independence is
resolved before fitting because alternate reductions of the same photons are
not repeated observations.

The release analysis downloads all 15 Patel et al. (2024) 55 Cnc e NIRCam
products: five independent eclipse visits, HANSOLO and stark alternate
reductions for each visit, and five two-band stark products. Over the common
3.95363–4.91020 μm interval, the median absolute HANSOLO–stark band-mean
difference is 64.03 ppm. Descriptive random-effects fits require 14.11 ppm
additional inter-visit scatter for HANSOLO and 37.14 ppm for stark. The stark
broadband series require 35.30 ppm at 2.12 μm and 41.03 ppm at 4.50 μm.

The archive products do not publish a spectral covariance matrix, so the
common-band uncertainty assumes independent bins and is not used as a physical
atmosphere likelihood. The comparison establishes reduction/visit
reproducibility bounds, not composition.

## HD 190360 model-adequacy control

A deliberately restricted circular NEID-only fit at the published 88.69 d
period gives K = 0.702 ± 0.048 m s⁻¹ and 4.435 m s⁻¹ residual WRMS. The
published multi-instrument/multi-planet context is approximately 1.48 m s⁻¹.
The restricted result is retained as evidence of model inadequacy and is not a
revised planet amplitude.
