## NETS III baseline diagnostics

All 41 public NETS III targets passed the minimum five-epoch diagnostic gate.

Across the 41-star sample:

| Quantity | Median |
|---|---:|
| RV epochs per target | 92 |
| time baseline | 1,001.95 d |
| quoted internal RV uncertainty | 0.34 m s⁻¹ |
| raw weighted RMS | 2.062 m s⁻¹ |
| run-demeaned weighted RMS | 1.794 m s⁻¹ |

The run-demeaning calculation is deliberately simple: each published survey run receives its own weighted mean before the residual RMS and exploratory GLS are calculated. The decrease in the sample median from 2.062 to 1.794 m s⁻¹ corresponds to a median 13.05% WRMS change. The median span between per-run weighted means is 2.10 m s⁻¹; 35 of 41 targets have a span above 1 m s⁻¹, and 13 of 41 show a WRMS change above 20% after run de-meaning. This establishes that between-run structure is numerically important at the precision scale of the survey.

Those between-run differences are **not** interpreted as pure instrumental zero points. They can contain genuine long-period companions, magnetic-cycle structure, sampling effects and calibration changes. Likewise, per-run de-meaning can absorb a real astrophysical signal. The statistic is therefore used as a sensitivity diagnostic that motivates explicit era-aware joint modelling rather than as a correction to be applied blindly.

Forty stars had enough matched S-index measurements for the simple weighted linear activity diagnostic. An in-sample one-covariate fit reduced WRMS for all 40. This is **not** interpreted as evidence that all stars are activity dominated: adding a fitted covariate is expected not to increase the weighted least-squares residual sum. The scientifically relevant test is held-out recovery and bias of injected/known Keplerian signals, which is why the pipeline treats activity correction as a model to validate rather than a cleaning step.

## HD 190360 model-adequacy control

The public NETS IV table contains 93 NEID measurements over 1,266.84 d.

A deliberately restricted model was evaluated at the published 88.69 d period using:

- a circular sinusoid;
- NEID data only;
- a linear trend;
- instrument/group offsets where provided;
- quoted formal uncertainties.

This simple model returned:

- (K = 0.702) m s⁻¹;
- formal WLS uncertainty (0.048) m s⁻¹;
- residual weighted RMS (4.435) m s⁻¹.

The published multi-instrument/multi-planet context reports a semi-amplitude around 1.48 m s⁻¹. The project **does not** interpret the simplified 0.702 m s⁻¹ value as a revised planet amplitude. Instead, the difference is retained as a useful model-adequacy control: a fixed-period, NEID-only, circular-plus-trend model can give a precise-looking but incomplete answer when additional planetary, activity and long-baseline structure is omitted.

This is exactly the failure mode the project is designed to expose.

## Atmospheric spectroscopy census

The live Atmospheric Spectroscopy metadata snapshot contained 1,826 spectrum records for 289 planets.

Multiplicity audit:

| Criterion | Planets |
|---|---:|
| ≥2 spectrum records | 245 |
| ≥2 publications | 149 |
| ≥2 instruments | 172 |

These counts define a substantial candidate set for repeatability analysis. They do not yet identify 245 independent repeated observations because the archive can preserve alternative reductions of the same observations as separate spectrum records. Independence must be established from publication/provenance metadata before the random-effects reproducibility model is applied.

The deterministic end-to-end control recovered:

- injected (K=2.4) m s⁻¹ as (2.400) m s⁻¹;

- an injected near-IR/optical amplitude ratio of 0.7 as 0.700;

- positive extra-scatter detection for deliberately overdispersed repeated measurements.

Those values are software validation outputs, not astrophysical measurements.
